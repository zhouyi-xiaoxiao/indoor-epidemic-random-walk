# Random-walk SIR models of indoor epidemics

Code, data and LaTeX source of

> Xiaoxiao Zhouyi, *Random-walk SIR models of indoor epidemics: reproduction numbers, discrete individuals and
> pre-specified tests against outbreaks, contact records and tracer measurements* (2026).
> [`paper.pdf`](paper.pdf), with Supplementary Material [`supplement.pdf`](supplement.pdf)

Version 1.2.0 (file `VERSION`).

## What the paper shows

The model is a susceptible–infective–recovered (SIR) epidemic among people who perform independent random walks
on a lattice of floor cells, with cell-dependent mobility and infection efficiency.

1. **Mean-field reproduction number.** The basic reproduction number is the spectral radius of a next-generation
   matrix built from the Laplace-transformed lattice propagator. For same-cell transmission the paper proves,
   from standard facts on non-negative matrices, its bounds, its limits and its monotone decrease in mobility,
   and a lower bound contributed by any high-risk zone; a counter-example shows that the lower bound and the
   monotonicity can fail for other contact kernels. In a closed room with uniform efficiency R0 does not depend
   on size, shape or mobility, for every contact kernel. A leak at rate μ_leak replaces γ by γ + μ_leak for
   uniform efficiency and the same-cell kernel, and for every kernel when the dwell time does not depend on
   position; with non-uniform efficiency and the same-cell kernel a leak gives two-sided bounds; for other
   kernels and a localised door the formula and both bounds can fail. Most of these results are known for
   epidemic patch models and are credited as such.
2. **Discrete individuals.** Following the two-walker theory of transmission (Kenkre and Sugaya; Giuggioli and
   Sarvaharman), infection is a partially absorbing defect of the two-walker process. With the pair hazard
   normalised by occupancy this gives the exact expected number of first-generation secondary cases of a lone
   index case and a closed form for homogeneous periodic rooms and the infinite floor (within 1 % for rooms with reflecting walls in the
   cases tested), and explains why counted secondary cases fall below R0 at low occupancy and low mobility (2.27 against 5.11
   in the office scene at the default mobility scale). This pair-level number is not a threshold quantity.
3. **Exact stochastic simulation** of four scenes (office, supermarket, classroom, metro carriage): outbreak
   probabilities, attack rates, the effects of barriers, occupancy and schedules, and the consequences of
   assumed reductions of infection efficiency (ventilation, masks). In a closed room the spatial effects are
   large only at very low mobility; at realistic walking mobility a closed room is nearly well mixed. Leaks
   through a localised door are the exception.
4. **Confrontation with data.** The model and its identifiable components are tested against 38 records of
   documented outbreaks in two datasets (18 of them enter the two out-of-sample tests of distance gradients and
   four more the level checks and negative controls of the first test), RFID contact records from six
   indoor settings, tracer measurements, and an influenza epidemic in 29 schools. Each of the five primary tests
   follows a protocol fixed before the model under test was run on its data (for the tracer analysis, on
   outbreaks whose outcome under the other models was already known). The protocols were written with the data
   known, were frozen by file hash on the author's machine (self-attested) and were **not lodged with a
   registry**; the paper calls them pre-specified, although their files are named `PREREGISTRATION.md`.
   Comparisons added after the results were known are marked as post hoc in the register of all tests
   (Supplementary Table S39). The outcome:
   * **No general agreement.** The model with transmission only between people on the same lattice site is not
     supported, and the contacts generated by its walk fail against recorded contacts in six of six settings,
     no better than a well-mixed model.
   * An extension with a diffusing airborne term predicts the decline of risk with distance better than a
     well-mixed model in the second outbreak test (log-score gain +14.35 on five held-out SARS-CoV-2 events),
     but not better than a constant near/far risk ratio: pooled over the five events the pre-specified
     comparison favours the constant ratio (−9.15), nine-tenths of it on one event whose near zone its source
     chose on the same data, and on four held-out flights the two are statistically indistinguishable. A post-hoc
     exponential decay with no physics in it does as well. No mixing coefficient is common to a class of
     settings, and coefficients fitted to outbreaks are not measurements of air mixing.
   * What the data support, and what the model shares with simpler ones: a smooth decline of risk with distance
     and, between zones, transmission dominated by the class (a weak pass).
   * Attack-rate levels are fitted, not predicted. Heterogeneous mobility, the defining feature of the model,
     was not tested: no dataset with a measured mobility field was available.

| `main.tex`, `supplement.tex`, `macros.tex`, `sections/`, `figures/`, `refs.bib` | LaTeX source (`sections/m*.tex`: main text; `sections/supp_*.tex`: Supplementary Material). Every figure exists with English (`_en`) and Chinese (`_zh`) labels |

| `notes/VERIFICATION.md` | how the results were checked, and what each check found |

| `code/*.py` | article-level scripts: one per section, table or figure; they read the stored results and write `data/*.json`, `data/*.tex` and `figures/*` |
| `code/bsc_theory/` | mean-field theory: next-generation matrices on lattices, random-instance tests of the theorems, worked examples; `verify/` holds the re-checks |
| `code/bsc_sim/` | exact event-driven simulator (`src/bsc_sim/gillespie.c`, Python front end, reference implementation, deterministic solvers), scene files, one script per experiment; `verify/` holds a second, separately written simulator and its checks |
| `code/bsc_outbreaks/` | construction and analysis of the first outbreak dataset; `verify/` holds the re-computation of the re-check |
| `code/bsc_validation/` | the first outbreak test: protocol (`PREREGISTRATION.md`), power addendum, freeze record with file hashes, post-hoc log, models, scripts; `verify/` holds the re-implementation of the re-check |
| `code/bsc_validation2/` | the four further analyses, each with its protocol, freeze records, post-hoc log, code and a `README.md`: `a1_more_outbreaks/` (second outbreak test), `a2_contact_mobility/` (contact records), `a3_tracer_physics/` (tracer measurements), `a4_zone_level/` (schools, building by floor) |
| `code/checks/` | re-implementations of those four analyses with separately written code (the re-checks) |
| `data/*` | article-level results (the numbers quoted in the text, with the file and key they come from) |
| `data/bsc_theory/`, `data/bsc_sim/`, `data/bsc_validation/`, `data/bsc_validation2/` | stored outputs of the research code |
| `data/bsc_outbreaks/` | **the first outbreak dataset**: `outbreaks.csv`, `outbreaks.json` (27 records, 51 fields), `sources.md` (31 sources and how each was read), auxiliary tables in `tables/` |
| `data/outbreaks2/` | **the second outbreak dataset**: `events.csv` (eleven records of ten outbreaks), `strata.csv` (persons at risk and cases by distance stratum), `seats.csv` (transcribed seat maps), `SOURCES.md` (reference and figure or table of origin of every number) |
| `data/tracer/`, `data/schools/`, `data/contacts/` | tracer tables; pupil-level influenza data of 29 schools and mixing shares; list of the contact datasets (URL, SHA-256) — each with a `SOURCES.md` |

`code/bsc_<name>/data` (and `code/bsc_theory/results`, `code/bsc_outbreaks/outbreaks.*`, `sources.md`, and the
result and data directories under `code/bsc_validation2/`) are **symbolic links** into `data/`, so that the
research code finds its files through the relative paths it was written with. Git stores them as links; on Windows
enable `core.symlinks` (or use WSL) before cloning.

Traceability: every quantitative statement in `main.tex`, `supplement.tex` and `sections/*.tex` is followed by a
comment `% src: <file> (<key>)` naming the results file it comes from. Numbers that come from a re-check rather
than from a results file of the research code are in `data/checks/*.json`, and the comments point there.

## Reproducing the results

See [`reproduce.md`](reproduce.md). In short:

```sh
python -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt            # numpy, scipy, matplotlib, mpmath, pandas, pillow; a C compiler is needed
python code/plan_theory_checks.py          # mean-field numbers on the four scenes (a few minutes)
python code/plan_fig_theory.py             # mean-field figures
python code/v2_numbers.py                  # numbers and table bodies of the further analyses, from the stored results
python code/v2_figures.py                  # their figures
latexmk -pdf main.tex && latexmk -pdf supplement.tex && latexmk -pdf main.tex
```

All stochastic results are stored in `data/` with the seeds that produced them; re-running every simulation
takes 3 to 4 hours on one core and less than 1.5 GB of memory.

## How the results were checked

Each part of the work was re-checked with separately written code, within the same project and on the same
machine; this is not an external or third-party verification. The core re-implementations were written
separately; some auxiliary checks reused modules of the analyses (re-runs, kernel comparisons, added
comparisons). Where a re-check corrected a result, the corrected result is the one reported. What was checked and
what was found is in [`notes/VERIFICATION.md`](notes/VERIFICATION.md).

The protocol records (`PREREGISTRATION.md`, power addenda, freeze records, the frozen code files and the post-hoc
logs) and the protocol first proposed with the outbreak dataset (`code/bsc_outbreaks/PROTOCOL_PROPOSAL.md`) are
released as **redacted copies**: passages that do not concern the analyses of this article are omitted (marked
`[...]`) or reworded, and identifiers are renamed as in the rest of the repository. Every redacted file, the lines
that differ from the original and the kind of each change are listed in [`code/REDACTIONS.md`](code/REDACTIONS.md),
with the SHA-256 hashes of the original and of the copy. The hashes in the freeze records refer to the unredacted
originals, which are kept by the author and can be provided to the editor and referees; the self-attested timing
evidence of the protocols can therefore not be checked from this repository alone. Where the protocol records
speak of a "verifier" they mean the re-check described above, not an independent
party; "the analyst" means the first analysis; both passes were made by the author. "The memo" means, in these
records, the working notes of an analysis, which are not distributed; their results are reported in the article.

## Third-party material

* The seat coordinates in `data/bsc_outbreaks/tables/park2020_callcentre_seats_digitized.csv` and the onset
  curve in `park2020_callcentre_epicurve_digitized.csv` were digitised from Figs 1 and 2 of Park et al.,
  *Emerging Infectious Diseases* 26(8), 2020, doi:10.3201/eid2608.201274, and are derived from those figures.
* `data/bsc_outbreaks/tables/hu2021_train_attack_matrix.csv` is transcribed from the percentages printed by
  Hu et al., *Clinical Infectious Diseases* 72(4), 2021, doi:10.1093/cid/ciaa1057; the cell counts are a
  reconstruction (Supplementary Section S6).
* `data/bsc_outbreaks/tables/li2021_restaurant_tables.csv` is transcribed from Li et al., *Building and
  Environment* 196, 2021, doi:10.1016/j.buildenv.2021.107788, and Lu et al., *Emerging Infectious Diseases*
  26(7), 2020, doi:10.3201/eid2607.200764.
* The outbreak records are numbers transcribed from the 31 sources listed in `data/bsc_outbreaks/sources.md`,
  each cited in the article. Copies of the source texts and figures are not redistributed; the scripts that
  fetch them (`code/bsc_outbreaks/scripts/fetch_*.py`) need network access.
* The seat maps and stratum counts of the second dataset (`data/outbreaks2/`) were transcribed by eye from the
  figures and tables of the sources listed in `data/outbreaks2/SOURCES.md`.
* The contact records (SocioPatterns; Creative Commons licences, some of which exclude commercial use) are
  **not redistributed**. `code/download_contact_data.py` downloads the ten files used and checks their
  SHA-256 (`data/contacts/datasets.csv`).
* `data/tracer/kinahan_*.csv` are extracts of the workbooks published by Kinahan et al. (2021) on figshare
  (doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.13537349.v1, 10.6084/m9.figshare.13537319.v1; CC BY 4.0). The other tracer tables
  were read or digitised from the figures of Ou et al. (2022) and Woodward et al. (2022); see
  `data/tracer/SOURCES.md`.
* `data/schools/matsumoto_students.csv` is a tabular copy of the anonymised pupil-level data published by
  Endo et al. (2021) under the MIT licence (`data/schools/LICENSE_matsumoto.txt`).
